# D-049: Local-calendar Revisit and reminder-safe boundary

- Date: 2026-07-24
- Owners: product, architect, backend, frontend, qa_security, localization, operations
- Related tasks: RIT-044, RIT-045
- Evidence references: docs/codex/rituvia-production-2026-07-23/01_PRODUCT_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md, docs/codex/rituvia-production-2026-07-23/02_UI_FIDELITY_CONTRACT.md, docs/codex/rituvia-production-2026-07-23/04_DOMAIN_MODEL_AND_DATABASE.md, docs/codex/rituvia-production-2026-07-23/05_API_CONTRACTS.openapi.yaml, docs/01_PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md, docs/02_USER_EXPERIENCE.md

## Context

The production API sketch represents a revisit as one UTC date-time, while the approved product
experience asks the owner to choose a return day and requires time-zone, quiet-hours, and reminder
controls. Treating that day as a fixed UTC instant can move it to the previous or next local day
during daylight-saving changes or travel. RIT-045 separately owns consented reminder delivery, so
RIT-044 must not create a sendable channel or imply that saving a revisit activates a message.

The existing intention stores an optional local revisit date and IANA time zone with reminders
fixed off. That date remains a non-authoritative handoff seed until an explicit Revisit is
created. RIT-044 needs a durable resource that can preserve the original intention/action,
survive later intention edits, and hold an encrypted factual comparison without reframing events
as proof of a prediction.

## Decision

Store Revisit scheduling as an exact local calendar date plus IANA time-zone identifier. Support
`next_day`, `seven_days`, and `custom` choices; resolve presets from the database-observed local
date, reject historical/out-of-retention dates, and determine display-only due state by comparing
the stored local date with the database clock in the stored time zone. The date is an invitation,
not a forced unlock: the owner may complete a scheduled Revisit early, on time, or late.

Snapshot the current owner-scoped v2 intention text, small action, and intention revision into
resource-bound authenticated encryption when scheduling. Bind v1 only to that intention; automatic
ritual or journal linking is deferred until a composite owner/resource chain can make every link
non-ambiguous and database-enforced.

Keep reminder preference exactly `none` and channel exactly null in RIT-044. Permit an optional
owner-controlled quiet-hours interval to be stored as a future delivery preference, while stating
that no reminder is active and emitting no outbox job, email, push, SMS, webhook, or analytics
event. RIT-045 must obtain any required approved consent copy and add a delivery adapter before a
channel can become active.

Allow a scheduled revisit to be rescheduled, completed with encrypted factual reflection and
optional non-clinical outcome tags, archived, or soft-deleted. A completed revisit may be archived
or soft-deleted; archived permits only soft deletion. Parent-intention soft deletion immediately
hides the Revisit. Snapshot and completion ciphertext retain independent key versions, and an
append-only operation ledger owns idempotency without making a mutable resource row the replay
authority. Every mutation is owner-scoped, revision-checked, and CSRF-protected.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Benefits | Costs/risks | Why not selected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Store only one UTC instant | Matches the skeletal OpenAPI | Can shift the intended local day across DST/time zones | Preserve the chosen calendar day |
| Reuse only intention.revisit_date | No new table | Cannot complete, archive, compare, or preserve original words | Revisit needs its own lifecycle |
| Auto-link ritual or journal | Enriches the comparison | Current schema lacks a safe composite ownership chain | Bind only the intention in v1 |
| Activate email reminders now | Immediate delivery | Crosses consent, copy, operations, and production-email gates | RIT-045 owns delivery |
| Store completion reflection in plaintext | Simpler search | Violates private-content classification | Encrypt resource-bound prose |

## Consequences

The browser can show the exact return day without UTC drift, and a future worker can evaluate due
rows without private text. Quiet hours are durable but inert until a separately approved reminder
preference exists. The completed comparison remains tied to what the user originally wrote rather
than a later edited intention. The append-only operation ledger preserves replay evidence across
later revisions and deletion.

This adaptation intentionally narrows the production OpenAPI `scheduledFor` date-time into a
server-authoritative local-date/time-zone contract. External API publication must reflect the
implemented version before production activation.

## Validation

Validation requires strict schedule/mutation/resource parsing, leap-day and DST-zone vectors,
future/retention boundaries, same-key replay and changed-key conflict, one open schedule per
owner/intention, optimistic reschedule/complete/archive/delete races, owner isolation, independent
snapshot/completion key-version substitution failures, parent-deletion hiding, exact encrypted
snapshots, no reminder/outbox writes, least privilege, logical restore, and production-artifact
browser coverage for presets, custom date, quiet hours, offline retry, early/on-time/late
completion, archive, delete, keyboard, RTL, 320px, reduced motion, axe, console/network, and
private-storage canaries.

## Rollout and rollback

Rollout is local-only through additive contracts, table, routes, and the private Revisit page.
Rollback disables the new routes and UI while leaving additive rows unread; no historical
intention, ritual, or journal row is rewritten. Production email, notification consent, retention
changes, destructive deletion, deployment, and public launch remain separate owner gates.

## Sources and approvals

Sources: the owner-approved production pack, golden Revisit interaction, current reflection
contracts, and canonical product/privacy/accessibility requirements. This decision does not
approve production reminder copy, channel activation, transactional email, data-retention
changes, deployment, or public launch.
