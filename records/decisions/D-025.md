# D-025: Finite public trust-content boundary

- Date: 2026-07-17
- Owners: product, frontend, qa_security, localization
- Related tasks: RIT-012
- Evidence references: apps/web/app, apps/web/test, docs/00_PROJECT_CHARTER.md, docs/01_PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md, docs/02_USER_EXPERIENCE.md, docs/06_AI_INTERPRETATION_SAFETY.md, docs/10_SECURITY_PRIVACY_RELIABILITY.md, docs/12_CONTENT_GOVERNANCE.md

## Context

The first English shell explains the product in one page but cannot yet give a durable, linkable
account of methodology, safety boundaries, or privacy design. Putting all trust content into home
page anchors would become difficult to navigate and cite, while prematurely labeling repository
copy as legal terms or a crisis flow would cross explicit owner and qualified-review gates.

## Decision

Keep `/en` as the product-positioning home and add only `/en/methodology`, `/en/safety`, and
`/en/privacy` in this version. Back every visible string with typed English messages, render all four
routes on the server, generate route-specific metadata, and admit their HTML/RSC representations
through the same fail-closed `experience.public_shell` gate.

The methodology page distinguishes deterministic facts, curated content, AI explanation, user
reflection, limitations, and the finite product loop. The safety page describes prohibited product
behavior and agency safeguards but does not publish a locale crisis response. The privacy page is
visibly a product-design summary, not a legal privacy notice, and must not claim that unfinished
controls or infrastructure already exist.

## Alternatives considered

| Option                                 | Benefits                                                     | Costs/risks                                                                    | Why not selected                               |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Keep all content as home anchors       | Few route changes                                            | Thin explanations, poor linkability, growing home page                         | Does not meet the distinct public-page outcome |
| Publish legal privacy/safety terms now | Appears launch-complete                                      | Unapproved legal, retention, consent, crisis, and regional claims              | Crosses explicit human approval gates          |
| Add a CMS/content service              | Editorial flexibility                                        | Storage, authorization, publication workflow, and operations beyond this slice | Premature infrastructure for four static pages |
| Finite typed server-rendered pages     | Exact surface, crawlable HTML, no runtime content dependency | Requires route/metadata/proxy/build-policy expansion                           | Selected                                       |

## Consequences

Visitors receive clear, durable trust context without login, JavaScript, personal data, or external
resources. Navigation and metadata expand, but locale activation remains exactly English and the
server flag remains the sole activation boundary. Future approved legal terms, crisis resources,
localized content, structured data, and publishing workflow remain separate work and cannot be
inferred from these pages.

## Validation

Tests must prove exact route parsing, per-route canonical metadata, safe-off HTML/RSC coverage,
typed copy completeness/prohibited-claim checks, server-only rendering, local navigation, and
resource/build budgets. Real-browser review must cover semantics, keyboard/focus, mobile/reflow,
themes/media preferences, no-JavaScript readability, console, and network requests across all four
routes. Reconsider the static content model when an approved editorial workflow needs independent
publication or localization.

## Rollout and rollback

No production deployment or feature activation occurs. The pages remain behind the existing public
shell flag and inherit non-production `noindex`. Revert the RIT-012 commit and regenerate evidence
to roll back; there is no data migration or external state.
