## Context

The production pack requires deterministic, transparent Life Path, Birthday Number, and Personal
Year calculations, but numerology traditions differ on aggregation order, master-number handling,
name mappings, and calendar assumptions. The golden prototype demonstrates one method but its
browser clock and optional Latin-name path are not suitable backend authority. RIT-080 therefore
prepared a source-checked exact method and blocked engine readiness behind OWN-011.

## Decision

Approve OWN-011 option A for RITUVIA rule-set version `1.0.0`:

- Life Path sums every ASCII decimal digit of canonical zero-padded proleptic-Gregorian
  `YYYY-MM-DD`, then repeatedly sums result digits.
- Birthday Number begins with the validated Gregorian day-of-month integer, then repeatedly sums
  its digits.
- Personal Year sums every digit of zero-padded birth month, birth day, and an explicit
  four-digit target year. The engine never reads ambient browser/server time.
- Every reduction step preserves exact totals `11`, `22`, and `33`; all other totals greater than
  nine continue to one digit.
- V1 accepts no name, Expression Number, transliteration, non-ASCII script, or non-Gregorian
  conversion.
- English is the only reviewed content locale. The date arithmetic may be presented in later
  reviewed locales only after input normalization to canonical ASCII ISO date form.
- The method is a RITUVIA product convention adapted from selected Western numerology practices,
  not a universal, scientific, predictive, diagnostic, or identity-determining claim.

## Alternatives considered

| Option                                                    | Benefits                                                       | Costs/risks                                               | Why not selected          |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| A: whole-date digit aggregation with explicit target year | Matches approved prototype behavior while remaining replayable | Differs from component-first schools                      | Owner explicitly approved |
| B: reduce year/month/day components separately            | Common alternative convention                                  | Requires new vectors and differs from prototype           | Not selected by owner     |
| C: provide another reviewed method/source                 | Could name a different tradition precisely                     | Blocks implementation until exact rules and rights arrive | Not selected by owner     |

## Consequences

RIT-080 can mark the exact catalog approved and RIT-081 can implement one deterministic engine.
Stored or displayed results must bind catalog, rule-set, rule, reduction policy, engine, and schema
versions. A target year is always explicit, name input is rejected rather than normalized, and
localized UI cannot change the arithmetic. Future variants require separate versioned rule sets
rather than silent changes.

This approval does not activate an API, UI, persistence, AI interpretation, SEO publication,
additional locale, deployment, or public launch.

## Validation

The catalog readiness gate must pass only with the exact approval reference. RIT-081 must match all
worked vectors, expose formula steps, reject invalid/Unicode/localized inputs, cover leap-year and
master-number boundaries, prove deterministic behavior without time access, and pass architecture
and affected-package checks.

Reconsider only through a successor decision when reviewed source evidence or product requirements
justify another method. Existing persisted results must retain their exact versions.

## Rollout and rollback

The approval is repository-only. Rollout is RIT-080 catalog approval followed by the pure RIT-081
engine; RIT-082 separately owns public UI. Before any persisted result exists, rollback can remove
the unused engine and restore the catalog to non-active status. Once results persist, rollback
requires a forward-compatible successor version rather than rewriting historical calculations.
