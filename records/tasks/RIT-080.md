## Outcome

The repository contains one strict, versioned, reviewable English numerology rule catalog for
Life Path, Birthday Number, and Personal Year calculations, with visible reduction semantics,
worked examples, source and approval evidence, master-number behavior, and explicit locale,
calendar, script, and privacy boundaries ready for the later deterministic engine.

## Scope

Included: a pure `@rituvia/divination` content contract; an immutable Git-authored English catalog
adapted from the owner-approved production pack; exact source, editorial, rule-set, reduction,
calendar, locale, privacy, and worked-example records; structural readiness assessment; and
focused parser, reference, safety, and publication-boundary tests.

Excluded: arithmetic execution, accepting or storing birth dates or names, Expression/Name Number,
transliteration, non-Gregorian calendar conversion, API routes, database schema or migration,
interpretive prose, AI, UI, SEO publication, additional locales, deployment, and public launch.
RIT-081 owns the calculation engine, RIT-082 owns the calculator UI, RIT-083 owns bounded AI
interpretation, and RIT-084 owns reviewed public content.

## Owner decision request: OWN-011

The production pack approves numerology as a deterministic, visible-formula modality and the
golden prototype contains one concrete browser algorithm. It does not independently establish
that algorithm as the authoritative backend rule or resolve known variants. RIT-080 therefore
cannot honestly mark the rule set approved without one exact owner choice.

**Recommended option A — approve the prepared V1 method**

- Life Path: sum every ASCII decimal digit of the zero-padded proleptic-Gregorian `YYYY-MM-DD`
  birth date, then repeatedly sum result digits.
- Birthday Number: begin with the validated Gregorian day-of-month integer, then repeatedly sum
  its digits.
- Personal Year: sum every digit of zero-padded birth month, birth day, and an explicit four-digit
  target year; never read the browser/server clock inside the engine.
- At every reduction step, preserve exact totals `11`, `22`, and `33`; otherwise continue to one
  digit.
- Launch catalog content is English. Date arithmetic can be presented in later reviewed locales
  only after boundary normalization to canonical ASCII ISO date input.
- V1 does not calculate Expression/Name Number, does not accept names, does not transliterate, and
  does not convert non-Gregorian calendars.
- The method is labeled a RITUVIA product convention adapted from selected Western numerology
  practices, not universal, scientific, predictive, diagnostic, or identity-determining.

**Option B — component-first variant**

Reduce year, month, and day separately before combining them, while separately specifying whether
`11`, `22`, and `33` survive at component and final stages. This requires replacing the prepared
examples and golden-prototype-compatible method.

**Option C — provide another reviewed method**

Supply the exact authoritative formula, master-number policy, named tradition/source, rights, and
locale/name scope. The repository draft remains non-engine-ready until those materials are
reviewed.

The owner replied `批准 OWN-011 选项 A` on 2026-07-25. Option A is effective for rule-set version
`1.0.0`; changing aggregation, target-year, master-number, locale, calendar, or name scope requires
a successor decision and rule-set version.

## Risks and rollback

The catalog defines the RITUVIA V1 product method and does not claim a universal, scientific, or
historically exhaustive numerology system. Source and editorial metadata can prove repository
structure and recorded approval references, not external authenticity. Birth dates and names
remain sensitive even though this slice stores neither. The later engine must not infer a target
year from the host clock or silently transliterate names.

There is no migration, public route, feature flag, runtime consumer, retained data, provider,
deployment, or production activation. Roll back by removing the numerology catalog/parser/tests
and restoring the records and generated evidence. Persisted rule references, once introduced by a
later task, must be superseded rather than silently edited.
